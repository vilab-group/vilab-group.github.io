Dept. of Computer Science and Engineering · OO University

Image synthesis, held to two standards: looking right, and being right.

Visual Intelligence Lab builds generative models for images. A synthetic photograph only has to convince the eye. A synthetic scan has to be anatomically faithful enough to train on — and to trust. We work across both because the harder standard is where the interesting problems live.

01 Natural images

Controllable generation and editing at high fidelity — and giving the user a real handle on what changes and what stays.

02 Medical images

Synthesis where data is scarce and mistakes are expensive: anatomy that holds together, pathology that a clinician finds plausible.

Research

Research

Generating images is one problem. Generating images someone can act on is another.

SYN

Controllable image synthesis

Diffusion and flow-based generation where the user keeps a handle on the result — layout, identity, and attributes that stay put when everything else changes.

Diffusion modelsControllable generationImage editing
MED

Medical image synthesis

Generating scans for the cases the archive never collected: rare pathology, under-represented scanners, counterfactual 'what would this look like without the lesion'. Anatomy has to survive the generation, not just the eye test.

Anatomy-faithfulCounterfactualData scarcityPrivacy
FID

Faithfulness and evaluation

FID tells you an image looks plausible, not that it is correct. We work on measures of anatomical and semantic fidelity, and on catching the hallucinations that a perceptual metric rewards.

EvaluationHallucinationUncertainty
USE

Synthetic data for downstream tasks

What synthesis is ultimately for: training detection, segmentation and anomaly models when real labels run out — including applied settings such as industrial inspection and remote sensing.

Data augmentationAnomaly detectionSegmentationTransfer
